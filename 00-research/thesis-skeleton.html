Where this is going

One page: what the papers found, what I found, where they differ, and what comes next. Revised monthly.
Published

October 7, 2026

This page is the running answer to “what is the thesis about”. It grows out of O2; paper numbers are that page’s.

The question

A model has information about itself: what it knows, how sure it is, what it can and cannot do, what it did before. When does that information become a guide to what it does next, and does it update when the facts change? Does acting on it need one joined-up picture of the self, or just a few narrow signals?

Where the papers left it

Plenty of data on whether models have self-knowledge (the confidence papers, [1] to [5]), almost none on whether they act on it ([6], [7]). The papers that do show self-relevant information driving action are the situational ones ([17], [19]): “I am being watched” changes behaviour; “I will get this wrong” mostly does not.

What I think is true so far

Each line: what the papers found, what I found, what differs, and push backs to my ideas

1. Telling a model the stakes does nothing

  • Papers: [6] Wang: models do not abstain more when a mistake costs more.
  • Probe bench: same result, seven models, penalty 1 to 64 stated in the prompt. At 64, every model scored below a model that always asked.
  • Counterfact: a model that moves once consequences are felt rather than stated. Nothing ive seen tests that, run batch with consqeunces then seocnd batch feed consquences into propmt or feefback loop within a probe?

2. Models act on their confidence; the confidence is what fails

  • Papers: [21] Barkan: the acting side works, the knowing side is wrong. [3] Xiong: stated confidence is overconfident. [10] Needham: ranking problems correctly (discrimination) and stating the right number (calibration) come apart.
  • Probe bench: across fourteen model-task cells, how much a model asks on weak problems tracks how far its confidence drops on them. Llama 8B ranks its problems correctly and still says 72 where 25% is true: Needham’s split in one model.
  • Counterfact: a model whose confidence drops on weak problems and whose asking does not.

3. Two kinds of knowing: not shown, and withdrawn for now

  • Papers: [1] Kadavath: P(IK), confidence before answering, and P(True), confidence in an answer already given, are different quantities. Not yet in the review: CALIBER trains the two separately; two further papers show that reasoning changes stated confidence, one of them on fact retrieval.
  • Probe bench: it looked as if the split showed up in which problems a model declines: take away the working and Llama 8B’s aim on maths went while Gemini’s aim on facts stayed. The two checks killed it. Without working Llama 8B solves 3% of those problems, so asking on 96% of them was right for that regime, and there is no strong set left to aim at. Gemini on maths without working keeps its aim (+31). What survives: both models’ confidence fell when the working was taken away, the right direction, still far too high (Llama 71/87 to 45/54, Gemini 88/100 to 71/90).
  • Counterfact: a model with real ability in both regimes whose aim differs between them. Llama 8B cannot give it on maths. Needs a task it can half-do without working.

4. No model has shown it can update its estimate of itself

  • Papers: none in the review tests whether the estimate moves within a task. [16] Betley shows models can report behaviours trained into them, which is self-knowledge at training time maybe but not updating in use.
  • Probe bench: three results now, all the wrong way. Showing Llama 8B its own disagreeing attempts lowered the confidence gap rather than sharpening it. Telling it its real record on the exact question (“right 2 of 10”) doubles its gap, but a shuffled number moves it about half as much, so it is partly obeying the line; GPT-4o mini ignores the line entirely (asks 3%, confidence 92, same as shuffled, same as baseline). Giving it a calculator changed nothing real (18%/61% right with, 19%/62% without, no problem flipped weak to strong) and still raised its before-working confidence on weak problems from 61 to 86. Gemini, the one model with a monitor of its own, drops it when handed a number: under a shuffled record its gap by real ability falls from +31 to +10 and its asking follows the handed number. With the calculator Gemini’s capability did not change either (its GSM-Hard failures are set-up errors, not arithmetic) and its estimate correctly stayed put. The one real capability change so far is a reference note holding the fact on TriviaQA: 34 of Gemini’s 39 weak questions flip to strong, its before-working confidence on them goes 66 to 100 and its asking 35% to 0%. A note about a different fact sends it the other way, to 13, and to 29 on questions it knows anyway (88 with no note) while it still gets them right. So the estimate reads what is in front of it, up or down, more than what it knows. Whether it falls back when the capability is withdrawn: see the withdrawal line. Under the shuffled record both models add the handed number to their own sense without checking one against the other: two self-relevant facts in play at once, not reconciled.
  • Update loop, run: Llama 8B answering the same ten problems three times over with the outcome and running score fed back after each. Real feedback makes it ask more and state less confidence than shuffled feedback does, but its aim is no better: asking on the problems it got wrong first time minus the ones it got right is +8 with real feedback and +6 with shuffled. Feedback changed the volume, not the aim, as predicted. Then the retrieval check: the same round-1 outcome repeated one line above the decision takes the gap from +8 to +79 (asks on 80% of the problems it got wrong first time, 1% of the ones it got right); no feedback at all gives +7, about the same as feedback left in the history. It does not fetch its own history. The fault is retrieval, not updating; the estimate updates question by question when the fact is adjacent, and only then.
  • Withdrawal, run: notes in phase 1, announced gone for phase 2 on new questions. Gemini’s estimate is not sticky, it drops when told the notes are gone, but it drops on everything: confidence 42 and asking 67% on questions it still gets right every time, against 72 and 33% in the control. The aim the control keeps is gone. Replicated at 16 conversations per arm with a third phase where the notes come back: down to 47 on questions it knows (control 75), then straight back to 100 on everything. Fourth time the announcement moved the estimate and the real ability did not.
  • Counterfact: a before-working confidence that falls back on the questions that lose a capability and not on the ones that keep it, or an aim that sharpens under real feedback and not under shuffled.

What I am testing now

The deferral pilot’s follow-ups are done: the two checks on line 3 (it did not survive), the record line, the calculator, the passage and the update loop on line 4, the withdrawal and its replication, the retrieval check, the distractor. The pilot is written up by finding on the Probe bench page, with the full record under it. Everything the black-box harness can ask has been asked. Next, the probe.

What comes next, anchored to what has been done

  • Updating. No paper in the review did this. The nearest outside it, to read before designing: Shinn (Reflexion, learning from episodic feedback in context) and Huang (“LLMs cannot self-correct reasoning yet”). The question for line 4 is whether a model’s estimate of itself follows its own outcomes, not whether its answers improve.
  • Inside the model. [7] Li found an internal signal that beats the stated confidence. [15] Nayan found that where you intervene matters: one layer inert, three causal. [20] Baek separated two mechanisms with the same behaviour by which internal features fired. Those three are the method for asking whether line 2’s bottleneck is the signal or the report of it, and whether line 3’s two kinds of knowing look different inside.
  • More than one fact at once. [19] Meinke put several self-relevant facts in an environment and the model acted on them, agentically. [15] Nayan showed that evaluation awareness is not one capability but several. [20] Baek showed the narrowest process, style-matching, can produce the whole behaviour. Together they set the test for O2’s second clause: does the model combine ability, tools, history and situation, or key on one? Everything I have so far moves on one input at a time. Code is the domain for this and it is the last thing to build.

What I am not claiming

Anything about frontier models, anything about mechanism, anything about training, and nothing smaller than about ten points. The pilot is a pilot.

Parked

The description problem: a system that cannot change the terms it describes itself in cannot update past those terms. Possibly a reading of the updating study. Not touched until that study has data.
