Q3: Can we map and understand the internal reasoning pathways of AI agents?

Intuition

Current AI systems are largely treated as black boxes.

We observe:

  • input
  • output
  • behaviour

but the internal computational process that produced the result remains difficult to interpret.

If we could map how information flows through an agent during reasoning, we may be able to understand:

  • why an error occurred,
  • why two agents diverged,
  • which internal pathways lead to successful decisions,
  • and how reasoning processes evolve.

Scientific Interpretation

A neural network transforms information through a sequence of high-dimensional representations.

During inference, an input is not directly mapped to an output. Instead, the information is progressively transformed through many internal computational stages.

For example:

  • The input is converted into numerical representations.
  • Different layers transform these representations into increasingly abstract features.
  • The model gradually builds an internal representation that supports the final decision.

Q2: Why do identical agents think differently?

Intuition

Imagine four scientists are given exactly the same research paper.

They have similar knowledge, the same information, and the same objective. However, each scientist may focus on different aspects:

  • one questions assumptions,
  • one analyses methodology,
  • one considers applications,
  • one searches for weaknesses.

The interesting question is not only why they produce different answers, but why their reasoning diverges despite starting from the same information.

Scientific Interpretation

Current multi-agent systems mainly expose:

  • prompts
  • messages
  • tool usage
  • final outputs

However, the internal computational processes that produce these outputs remain largely hidden.

Agents may diverge because of differences in:

  • latent representations
  • memory retrieval
  • attention patterns
  • stochastic generation
  • reasoning trajectories

The key question becomes:

How do internal computational processes shape the reasoning and behaviour of AI agents?

Q1: How does an agent know it should learn?

Intuition

Humans often recognise that their internal understanding is incomplete before receiving external feedback.

Rather than immediately changing behaviour after failure, they first detect a mismatch between their expectations and reality.

Conceptual Example

Imagine someone is given a cup with an unfamiliar shape. Their first attempt to hold it follows previous experience, but the interaction feels different. The mismatch between expectation and reality triggers exploration and adaptation.
